Purchasing Pipeline: Final Plan

Three days of investigation, six defects fixed and proven in production, and a design that got smaller every time you corrected it. This is the settled version: what the outcome is, what stands, what I am deleting, and the ordered list of what is left to do.

01

The outcome we are building toward

  1. Know what every live order actually is, without reconstructing it
    a number that is unique, a delivery date that means delivery, a scope that excludes history
  2. See production impact before it is too late to act
    which packs are at risk, why, what the next action is and whose it is
  3. Have status reach people without going through you
    a change stream and a portal, not a question
  4. Catch friction once and prove it is gone
    an edge case becomes a case, a case gets an action, an action closes only on a measured move

Everything below is measured against those four. Anything that does not serve one of them has been cut.

03

What I am deleting

You asked for less to maintain. Each of these is removed from the plan — three of them were my own additions, and one was a design I argued for before you corrected the premise.

Per-pack PO allocation
Cancelled entirely — table, split rule, mismatch flag. Ordering the BOM sum plus stock or contingency means ordered quantity is supposed to exceed the pack total. The flag would have fired on correct buying.
The eight pack-set decisions
Off the critical path. They belong to pack reporting, not to purchasing, and nothing waits on them.
Pack reference registry
Downgraded from a maintained alias table to a one-off cleanup plus a uniqueness rule. A registry is a thing to keep feeding; a clean-up is done once.
My pack GID resolver
Retired in favour of 06_normalise_pack_gid.py, which was written first, guards ambiguity properly and already exists. Two implementations of one rule is one too many.
Putting the pack GID in descriptions
Dropped on your instruction. The reference string stays the user-facing key.
Walkthrough subtasks B and C
They demonstrated the remainder and mismatch cases, which no longer exist. Cut to two subtasks: a clean order and a moved line.

And one correction to carry forward

I twice reported that no receipt event exists for project parts, reading Arrived Stock Qty as unpopulated. It is a staging field — written on book-in, consumed, cleared — so an empty snapshot is it working. The path is also deliberately not in use yet, pending correct stock mapping. The conclusion was right by accident and the reasoning was wrong; the reasoning is what would have misled the next decision.

Checking it also found a bug in my own change-detection code: _norm(x) or None collapsed a real zero to NULL, destroying exactly the 0 → value transition that a staging field is made of. Fixed, and 34 false events cleared.

The settled flow

DEMAND
  BOM import          task carries the TOTAL required
  pack ingest         adds per-pack breakdown + split marker to the task
                      (feeds the picking report — NOT the purchasing path)

PURCHASE
  task exists         a NEEDS item, a project order, or a line moved off another order
  reserve             instant, local, atomic        -> 21629-1597
                      suffix = project id, else today's date (day unpadded)
  you apply it        typed into the task name, exactly as now
  order lines         each part parented to the order becomes a po_lines row
  extra               ordered minus BOM required, with a reason: stock | contingency

MOVEMENT                                              (the ordinary week)
  unavailable         rebind to another order — allocation follows the PART
  made dependent      keeps its binding, gains a dependency; later ETA wins
  re-homed            project task or new task — still just a rebind

CONFIRM
  sent items sync     records the address the PO actually went to
  ledger              reserved -> confirmed

RECEIPT                                               [GATED — see below]
  arrived qty         staging field: set on book-in, consumed, cleared
  blocked on          correct stock mapping from the Golden Index BOM import

05

The critical path runs through the Golden Index

Your note that the arrived-quantity path is held pending correct mapping of parts coming off the shelf changes the priority order. That mapping is not a side task — it is the gate on the entire receipt half of the pipeline, and everything downstream of goods-in waits behind it.

What the gate currently measures

golden_index_rev6 holds 177 entries covering 367 part numbers, of which 100 carry an Asana task GID. Against 335 distinct live part numbers in Parts Control, 2 match. The index and the live work are, right now, almost disjoint sets.

That is not necessarily a fault — the index is a stock catalogue and most live work is project parts. But it does mean the coverage question has to be answered before the arrived path can be switched on, and nobody can answer it from the numbers alone.

The question that unlocks it: of the parts that come off the shelf on a live job, what share should be in the Golden Index and are not? That is a sampling exercise on one or two jobs, not a rebuild, and it is the smallest thing that tells you whether the gate is nearly open or a long way off.
