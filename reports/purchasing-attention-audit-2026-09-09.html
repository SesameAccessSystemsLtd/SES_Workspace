01

Executive finding

The purchasing pipeline is more complete than the documentation claims in some places and much less complete in one specific place. Demand capture works: every BOM that has been imported this year lands cleanly as part records, and six nightly mirrors keep a full copy of the Asana purchasing estate in ops.db. The failure is not at either end. It is at the single join in the middle.

There is no data link between a required part and the order that covers it. Across 2,761 part records, the field that would hold the purchase-order reference — parts_control.procurement_task_gid — is empty on every single row. Across 1,698 BOM requirement lines, bom_requirements.po_id is empty on every single row. The tables that would record receipt and allocation (po_lines, supplier_deliveries) exist with full schemas and contain zero rows, and have never contained any.

That single missing join is what forces you to be the integration layer. Because no system can answer "which order covers this part," the answer is reconstructed by a human reading task names, vendor spellings and Asana parentage. Every downstream question — is this order late, has it arrived, which build pack is now short, who should chase it — inherits that reconstruction, and inherits you with it.

Three consequences show up in the evidence, and they account for most of the recurring load:

1 · Status maintenance replaced status measurement Confirmed

The mirrored database says 392 purchase-order tasks are open, worth £167,855, with 241 more than 30 days past their due date. Your own live oversight page, generated the same morning, says 31 orders are genuinely awaiting confirmation. The gap is not an automation bug — the oversight page excludes completed legacy orders and the raw mirror does not. It means the fastest honest answer to "what is actually on order" currently requires a purpose-built script, and any answer taken from the database without that filter is wrong.

Evidence: procurement_tasks open-status query vs data/os_status/ops-oversight.html generated 2026-09-09 08:06.

2 · Design Feedback has become a vendor-data repair desk Confirmed

67% of Design Feedback tasks raised in 2026 are single BOM part lines of the shape "4 off 1597. 7110.E.014 — Sleeve bearing WSM-1618-08 Igus", up from 47% in 2025. Tracing one end to end: the importer set the vendor to Igus; you commented asking Design to change it to IGUS; you re-parented the part under the correct supplier order; two days later it came back marked NEED TO ORDER by Production. The project's own cause field is now meaningless — 1,216 of 1,248 tasks carry the non-cause value "Update — aids in the metric report" — so this work is invisible to any metric.

Evidence: task 1597.7110.E.014 activity feed 2026-09-07 → 09-09; Route cause distribution across all 1,248 tasks.

3 · Your judgement is captured, then discarded Confirmed

Corrective Actions are filled on 61% of the purchasing-addressed Delay Destroyer tasks, and they are genuinely good — supplier-continuity rules for refinished parts, fixings standardisation, second-source policy for mag brakes, the rule that any dependent task must route back to you. But the fields that would make them reusable are effectively empty: LEARINGS 12%, Requires De-Brief 8%, DAY LAG 6%. Actual Cost is filled on 99 tasks and sums to £480, so the cost case those actions were meant to build cannot be made. The reasoning is written down once, in free text, on a task that closes.

Evidence: custom-field fill rates across 212 purchasing-addressed Delay Destroyer tasks, 2019–2026.

The headline number for personal load: 579 of the 617 Asana tasks assigned to you are past their due date — 94%. A queue that is 94% overdue is not a queue; it has stopped carrying signal, which is why triage runs on top of it and why urgent things reach you by interruption instead.

You The join
Every dashed break above is closed by a person reading names and remembering context.

A second, quieter defect

The six mirror jobs run nine times a day and rewrite every column of every row each time, logging all columns as "changed" whether or not they were. db_write_log now holds 3.81 million rows — 769,043 in the last 30 days for a 4,085-row table — and is the main reason ops.db is 1.21 GB. The practical cost is not disk: it is that there is no change stream. Nothing can answer "which orders changed status since yesterday," which is precisely the question that would let status reach people without passing through you. Confirmed

03

System completeness assessment

Working — keep and depend on
  • Asana → ops.db mirroring. Six syncs, nine runs/day, current to 2026-09-09 09:01. Procurement tasks, parts control, build packs, stock, suppliers, invoices.
  • BOM import through to Parts Control. 100% BOM-line-to-part resolution on all four jobs traced. The May-2026 documented gap (bom_requirements never written) is closed.
  • Email ingest and human-gated approval. Live, idempotent, 2,769 emails de-duplicated, no unreviewed Asana writes.
  • Parts Control propagator. Ran on 8 of the last 10 working days, 1,700 audited stock-action propagations.
  • The oversight portal. ops-oversight, giles-worklist, bom-tracker regenerate daily and already do live-verified open-order reporting. This is the most under-used asset in the estate.
Nearly complete — one connection short
  • Overdue chase. Detection, certainty grading and draft generation all work. 11 drafts sit unsent; no send/track/close step exists.
  • Ordering queue. Grouping works nightly. The ordered transition has not fired since 2026-07-03 because PO raising happens outside the queue and nothing feeds the result back.
  • Supplier master. 431 suppliers with types, tags and 209 contacts — but keyed on descriptive Asana names ("AALCO Ltd - Aluminium and stainless steel") while parts carry short free text ("Aalco"). Only 5 of 119 part-vendor keys match the master.
  • Legacy BOM handover. 22 packs prepared, 1 committed. The prepare step runs; the commit step waits on you.
Unreliable — present but not trustworthy without verification
  • Open-order counts from the mirror. 392 vs 31 verified. Any figure taken without the live filter is wrong.
  • Vendor identity. 238 collision groups in procurement tasks (1,335 raw spellings → 779). 13 Parts Control "vendor" values are actually part descriptions ("2 off packs of 25", "4 off BS EN ISO 4168").
  • The qc_issues table. 9,065 rows, exactly matching the Invoices project's task count, with every impact and signed_off_status null. It appears to be an invoice mirror under a QC name. Inferred
  • Documentation. The SES_Brain capsules and the purchasing SKILL.md still route through .openclaw, retired in August. PATH_CONFIG.yaml honestly reports migration_state: in_progress.
Missing — no implementation at all
  • Part-to-order reference. The whole basis of every downstream answer.
  • Goods-in event for project parts. No receipt, no partial-delivery record, no shortage record, no QC-pass record.
  • Allocation. po_lines.allocated_qty exists as a column and has never held a value.
  • Change stream. No usable "what changed" feed despite 3.81M write-log rows.
  • Feedback write-back. Nothing carries a Design Feedback or Delay Destroyer conclusion into the next BOM, drawing or supplier record.

Falling volume is not evidence of a closing loop Confirmed

Recording quality fell alongside volume. 46% of 2026 Delay Destroyer tasks carry no REASON at all (51 of 111). Design Feedback's cause field collapsed into a single non-cause value. Where classification survives, it is stable year on year: Process Issue, Design — BOM issue, Parts — QC, Error in Ordering and Supplier Issue have been the top purchasing categories since 2023.

Resolution is slow and open items are very old Confirmed

Purchasing-addressed Delay Destroyer tasks take a median 58 days to close, mean 78, 90th percentile 141, maximum 1,183. Twelve remain open; the oldest has been open 1,266 days, two more over 900. Design Feedback closes faster — median 23 days — but 21 remain open including items from 2018, 2019, 2020 and 2021.

Recurrence is acknowledged but not acted on Confirmed

Of 212 purchasing-addressed delay tasks, 47 are explicitly marked "Has happened before" or "Yes happens frequently", 80 as "Not happening regularly", and 85 carry no assessment. There is no field, report or routine that reviews the 47 as a set. The one demonstrable permanent correction in the record — the steel-profile BOM task now generated with every import — was made because a pipeline changed, not because the project prompted it.

The cost case cannot currently be made Confirmed

The project's stated purpose, recorded in its own description, was to prove £68k of recoverable cost. Actual Cost is now populated on 99 of 212 purchasing tasks and totals £480. Whatever those numbers mean, they cannot support the argument the project exists to make.

10

Questions only you can answer

Each of these would change the findings or the priority order, and none can be settled from the environment.

  1. Is the 392-vs-31 gap tolerated or unnoticed? If the team already knows the mirror over-counts and works from the oversight page, leverage 03 gets easier and cheaper. If people are reading the raw counts, it is more urgent than ranked.
  2. Why did the ordering queue stop advancing on 3 July? Deliberate abandonment, a change in how you raise POs, or an unnoticed break? The fix is different in each case, and 117 stale lines are currently misleading.
  3. What does Actual Cost mean on a Delay Destroyer task? £480 across 99 tasks reads as hours, or as a field people gave up on. The answer decides whether item 09 is repair or retirement.
  4. Which of the 230 email-less suppliers actually matter? If it is thirty, contact capture is an afternoon and unblocks chase. If it is genuinely 230, the chase automation needs a different design.
  5. Is manual PO numbering negotiable in any form? Not automatic assignment — but could the system reserve a number that you confirm? That single change would let the part-to-order link be written at source rather than reconstructed.
  6. What is actually running the six nightly syncs? The only relevant scheduled tasks on this machine are failing stubs, yet the syncs demonstrably run. Until that is known, nothing built on top of them can be relied on. Unknown
  7. Of the 352 unclassified tasks, how many are genuinely yours? A rough split — half, a quarter, nearly all — decides whether item 08 is the top priority or the eighth.
